# Series starter

Optional. A series is a recurring promise to an audience. It can produce several formats without forcing them to look alike.

## Promise

[Series name, intended audience, recurring question, and what each installment should help them understand or do.]

## Boundaries

[Topics included and excluded. Distinguish firsthand testing from analysis of other people's reports. Never imply firsthand experience that has not happened.]

## Episode pattern

[Choose useful beats such as context, demonstration, evidence, interpretation, or next action. Set length, cadence, and review checkpoints only if needed. Do not require every episode to fit every beat.]

## Related formats

[For example, a spoken script for a video and a written post introducing it. Give each its own template and instructions, linked to this series.]

## Sources and production (optional)

[Required source types, dates, visuals, recording notes, or review steps. Narration should follow the actual visual order and add explanation. If demonstrating AI output, adapt to the observed result rather than promising an exact response.]

## Illustration

AI Model Trends could be a LinkedIn video series explaining one model development per episode. A script can cover the question, available evidence, a demonstration when available, and the practical implication. Its companion LinkedIn post can share one useful finding and a link to the video. Both use the creator's approved voice and series context; the post does not copy the spoken script or inherit its recording directions. This example supplies no fixed audience, vendor list, tone, length, or personal experience.
