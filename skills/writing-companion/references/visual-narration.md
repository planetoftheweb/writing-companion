# Visuals and spoken narration

Guidance version: 2026-10-10.1

Use this method for spoken scripts with visuals. Apply the creator's approved voice, audience and project rules to both the visual copy and narration. It is not a universal personality or a requirement for articles and private notes.

Treat a visual and its explanation as one section: a short setup when needed, the visual, immediate narration of its points in reveal order, then the implication or transition. Do not stack unrelated visuals before explaining them or leave the explanation several paragraphs away.

Give each visible point a matching spoken beat. Use the same names and sequence so the presenter can advance the visual while reading. Short bold lead-ins or bullets can help recording when the creator wants them. Explain what each point means, why it matters, or a supported example; do not merely repeat every label. Keep visual labels brief and familiar, with detail in the narration. Personal examples belong only where supported and appropriate for the intended public audience.

For timelines, match the actual dates and event order. For conversations, explain the exchange beat by beat and distinguish the person from the bot. For diagrams, explain the relationships shown rather than inventing significance for decorative shapes. A visual is not evidence: do not turn unsupported generated labels into factual claims.

Respect edit scope. For a visual-only change, update only the visual, including its title or subtitle when requested; do not rewrite adjacent narration. For narration-only work, preserve the visual. When asked to create or revise a whole section, align the visual and narration together. When adding a visual to fixed prose, fit the visual to that prose or identify the mismatch outside the draft. Preserve structured syntax and existing facts, dates, links and quotations.

Before returning a section, compare visible points against the immediate narration: every important point has a matching explanation, the order agrees, the wording follows the approved voice, and no unsupported detail was added. Flag missing source material instead of filling it with technical guesses. Keep production notes distinct from spoken words.
